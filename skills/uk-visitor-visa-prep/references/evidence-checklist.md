# Evidence Checklist Guide

Select evidence for the applicant's circumstances. This is not a mandatory-document list and more documents do not automatically make a stronger application.

## Core identity and status

- Valid passport or travel document covering the intended stay
- Evidence of legal residence where applying, if it is not evident from the passport
- Saved or submitted application form and appointment/upload records, when available

## Purpose and trip

- Credible itinerary matching the declared dates, budget, and permitted activities
- Intended accommodation details stated accurately as booked or unbooked
- Relevant invitation or event evidence when the visit is not ordinary tourism

Hotel and flight bookings are not substitutes for evidence of circumstances and funding. Do not imply that refundable or unbooked arrangements are confirmed.

## Applicant finances

- Recent statements clearly identifying the account holder and showing access to relied-upon funds
- Evidence of income or regular support where declared
- Explanations and source evidence for material unusual credits
- A clear connection between accessible funds, regular commitments, and the trip budget

## Sponsored visits

- Sponsor statement describing what is paid, for whom, how, and up to what scope
- Evidence of the relationship
- Sponsor statements and income or savings evidence showing that support is affordable after the sponsor's own commitments
- Sponsor's UK immigration status only when relevant, such as a UK-based sponsor

## Personal circumstances and return reasons

Use only current and relevant evidence, such as:

- employer letter with role, salary, start date, approved leave, and contact details
- education-provider letter confirming enrolment, programme dates, expected completion, and approved absence where applicable
- ongoing lawful residence, tenancy, dependent family responsibilities, or business activity
- previous passports showing compliant travel history

A future graduation can support the overall circumstances when documented, but it is not a guarantee and should not be described as a legal bond.

## Translation quality

For each non-English or non-Welsh document, check:

- complete translation, not only highlighted lines
- accuracy confirmation
- date of translation
- translator's real full name and signature
- translator's contact details
- original-language pages included and correctly ordered

## Status definitions

- `ready`: readable, complete, current, and consistent with the form
- `review`: available but ambiguous, weak, stale, incomplete, or conflicting
- `missing`: necessary to support a material claim but not supplied
- `not applicable`: irrelevant to this applicant and visit purpose
